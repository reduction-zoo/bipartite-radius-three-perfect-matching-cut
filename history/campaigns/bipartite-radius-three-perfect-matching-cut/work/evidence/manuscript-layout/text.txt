Perfect Matching Cut on Bipartite Graphs of Radius
                      Three

                              Research manuscript — awaiting expert review

Abstract
We give a deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction from positive Not-All-Equal 3-SAT to
Perfect Matching Cut on connected simple bipartite graphs of radius at most three. The construction
combines a three-choice selector with binary variable interfaces, all sharing one center edge. Each
interface uses its required crossing edges internally, so clause attachments enforce color equality
without consuming additional crossing edges. For a nonempty formula with 𝑛 variables and 𝑚 clauses,
the output has 4 + 8𝑛 + 160𝑚 vertices and 3 + 14𝑛 + 304𝑚 edges. We prove both directions and
give polynomial-time recovery of a satisfying source assignment from every valid target coloring. The
result addresses the radius-three gap between known radius-two tractability and radius-four hardness.
Computational checks support the implementation; the arbitrary-input claim rests on the general proof.

1. Introduction
A perfect matching cut partitions the vertices of a graph into two nonempty sets so that every vertex
has exactly one neighbor in the other set. This condition couples a local matching requirement with
a global partition. Its complexity can change when the graph has small radius, because a center then
constrains every vertex through a short path.
Lucke, Paulusma and Ries proved polynomial-time solvability for graphs of radius at most two [2,
Theorem 21]. Lucke established hardness on bipartite graphs of radius four and explicitly asked for the
radius-three case [1, Theorem 19 and Open Problem 2]. The radius-four construction uses cube clauses
and connectors. The construction here realizes signed exact-one clauses with selectors sharing a center
edge. Binary interfaces supply both a variable and its complement at distance two from the center.
Theorem 1. There is a deterministic polynomial-time Karp reduction from positive NAE-3-SAT to Perfect
Matching Cut on connected simple bipartite graphs of radius at most three. A deterministic polynomial-
time algorithm recovers a satisfying source assignment from every perfect-cut coloring of the constructed
graph. Consequently, Perfect Matching Cut is NP-complete on this graph class.
The main construction requirement is that an interface retain its meaning when attached to the rest of
the graph. We prove each relation with an upper bound of one internal crossing edge at its attachment
vertices. The proof then forces those vertices to use exactly one internally, leaving every added incident
edge monochromatic. This permits repeated occurrences of a variable without creating new copies of
its interface.

2. Definitions and source encoding
For a graph 𝐺 = (𝑉 , 𝐸), write 𝑁𝐺 (𝑣) for the neighbors of 𝑣. A perfect-cut coloring is a map 𝑐 : 𝑉 →
{0, 1} such that
                         |{𝑤 ∈ 𝑁𝐺 (𝑣) : 𝑐(𝑤) ≠ 𝑐(𝑣)}| = 1      for every 𝑣 ∈ 𝑉 .                      (1)

The color classes are automatically nonempty. The radius is the minimum, over vertices, of the greatest
distance to any vertex. All constructed graphs are finite, undirected and simple.
A positive NAE-3-SAT instance is a formula Φ over 𝑛 variables, with each clause containing three
distinct unnegated variables. Each clause must contain both truth values. Repeated clauses and unused
variables are allowed. This source is NP-complete [1, Theorem 18]. Let 𝐸(𝑙1 , 𝑙2 , 𝑙3 ) mean that exactly
one of the three signed literals is true.

Lemma 2 (robust selector). Suppose 𝑐(𝑢) = 0, vertices 𝐹 , 𝐴, 𝑑1 , 𝑑2 , 𝑑3 have exactly one internal
opposite neighbor, and vertices 𝑢, ℎ, 𝑇1 , 𝑇2 , 𝑇3 have at most one. Then 𝑐(ℎ) = 𝑐(𝐹 ) = 1, 𝑐(𝐴) = 0, exactly
one 𝑑𝑖 has color one, and 𝑐(𝑇𝑖 ) = 1 − 𝑐(𝑑𝑖 ). Each 𝑇𝑖 has exactly one internal opposite neighbor. Every one-
hot choice of the 𝑑𝑖 extends to such a coloring.
Proof. If 𝑐(𝐴) = 1, the crossing edge 𝐴𝑢 forces all 𝑑𝑖 to have color one. Each 𝑑𝑖 then needs exactly one
zero-colored neighbor among the other two 𝑇 vertices. Summing these three requirements gives twice
the number of zero-colored 𝑇 vertices equal to three, a contradiction. Thus 𝑐(𝐴) = 0. Since 𝐴 needs
exactly one opposite neighbor, exactly one 𝑑𝑖 , say 𝑑𝑘 , has color one.
The edge 𝑑𝑘 𝐴 already crosses, forcing 𝑐(𝑇𝑗 ) = 1 for every 𝑗 ≠ 𝑘. Each remaining 𝑑𝑗 has color zero and
sees one such one-colored 𝑇 vertex, so it forces 𝑐(𝑇𝑘 ) = 0. Vertex 𝐹 cannot have color zero because it
would have two opposite neighbors. It therefore has color one, uses 𝐹 𝑇𝑘 as its crossing edge, and forces
𝑐(ℎ) = 1. Now 𝑇𝑘 crosses only to 𝐹 ; each other 𝑇𝑗 crosses only to the remaining zero-colored 𝑑 vertex.
These colors also verify the converse for each choice of 𝑘. □
The lemma applies to the restriction of any full perfect-cut coloring: attachment vertices can only lose
crossing neighbors when outside edges are removed. It forces the center edge 𝑢ℎ to cross without
assuming that edge in advance.

each variable block has 𝑐(𝑝) = 𝑐(𝑣) and 𝑐(𝑟) = 𝑐(𝑤) = 1 − 𝑐(𝑝). Both occurrence ports already have one

both 𝑎 and 𝑞 as opposite neighbors, so both occurrences must be zero; this leaves 𝑞 without an opposite
neighbor. Therefore 𝑐(𝑞) = 1.

4. Clause composition and recovery
For the 𝑖th literal of a clause, connect its selector port 𝑇𝑖 to 𝑤𝑥 if the literal is 𝑥, and to 𝑣𝑥 if the literal
is ¬𝑥. The same occurrence pair can serve arbitrarily many clauses. The attachment rule is
                                  𝑇𝑖 𝑤𝑥   for 𝑙𝑖 = 𝑥,       𝑇𝑖 𝑣𝑥    for 𝑙𝑖 = ¬𝑥.                            (8)

Lemmas 2 and 3 force these edges to be monochromatic. In both cases, the color at the occurrence
endpoint is the complement of the literal value. Therefore 𝑐(𝑑𝑖 ) equals that literal value, and the selector
imposes exactly one true literal in the clause.
Proof of Theorem 1. First suppose Φ is satisfiable. Extend its assignment to Ψ by Equation 3. Color
each binary block according to its encoded bit, and color each selector according to the unique true
literal of its clause. The attachment edges are monochromatic. Lemmas 2 and 3 verify all private vertices,
while the shared vertices use only 𝑢ℎ and 𝑏𝑡 as their crossing edges. This gives a perfect cut of the
constructed graph.
Conversely, let a perfect-cut coloring of the constructed graph be given. For a nonempty source formula
there is at least one selector. After normalizing 𝑢 to zero, Lemma 2 forces ℎ to one, and Lemma 3 gives
a consistent bit for every variable. By Equation 8, every selector certifies a signed exact-one clause. The
decoded assignment satisfies Ψ and hence its restriction satisfies Φ. This applies to every target witness,
including color complements.
The executable labels 𝑢 by zero and the original variable port 𝑝𝑖 by 5 + 8𝑖, with original variables
indexed from zero. Recovery is the deterministic formula
                                    𝑔(Φ, 𝑦)𝑖 = 𝑦5+8𝑖 ⊕ 𝑦0           (0 ≤ 𝑖 < 𝑛).                             (9)

It checks the input witness on the reconstructed target and performs no source search or oracle call.
The target-domain and polynomial bounds are proved below. Finally, a coloring and the target graph
properties can be verified in polynomial time, establishing NP membership. □

4.1. Empty and invalid encodings
An empty formula maps to 𝐾2 ; recovery returns 𝑛 zero bits. The source encoding includes the explicit
list of variables, so unused variables cannot be hidden in a short binary parameter. Invalid source strings
map to 𝐶6 . This graph is connected, bipartite, and has radius three, but has no perfect matching cut:
such a cut would contain three edges of the cycle, whereas every cut crosses a cycle an even number of
times. Invalid recovery premises fail explicitly. These rules complete both algorithms on the specified
encodings.

The following layers give paths from 𝑢 and certify bipartiteness. Names range over all blocks of the
indicated type.

 Layer   Vertices
 0       𝑢
 1       ℎ, 𝑏, all 𝑎, 𝐴
 2       𝑡, all 𝑓, 𝑧, 𝑣, 𝑤, 𝐹 , 𝑑𝑖
 3       all 𝑝, 𝑟, 𝑞, 𝑇𝑖

Every edge in Equation 6, Equation 7 and Equation 8 joins adjacent layers, and every vertex above
layer zero has a neighbor in the preceding layer. Thus the graph is connected and bipartite, and every
vertex is within distance three of 𝑢. Private labels are distinct across blocks. The listed internal edges
are distinct, and each attachment has its own clause port, so no loop or duplicate edge is introduced,
even with repeated clauses.
For a nonempty source, the exact counts are
                               |𝑉 (𝐺)| = 4 + 8𝑁 + 8𝐶 = 4 + 8𝑛 + 160𝑚,

                               |𝐸(𝐺)| = 3 + 14𝑁 + 17𝐶 = 3 + 14𝑛 + 304𝑚.

The forward algorithm performs 𝑂(𝑛 + 𝑚) graph operations and emits 𝑂((𝑛 + 𝑚) log(𝑛 + 𝑚 + 1))
bits. Label arithmetic and parsing are polynomial in the source encoding length 𝐿; a conservative bound

It uses at most 𝑂((𝐿 + |𝑦|)2 ) bit operations and emits 𝑛 bits. Both maps are deterministic and use no
solver or persistent process state.

6. Scope of the result
The reduction supplies the radius-three hardness needed alongside the known radius-two algorithm
for Perfect Matching Cut on bipartite graphs. Its shared center and saturated clause ports are the mech­
anism that keeps every vertex within three layers. This result does not imply the same classification
for ordinary Matching Cut or Disconnected Perfect Matching, whose witness predicates differ from
Equation 1.
